Introduction: Scope & Objective

Goal: Build an image-based visibility predictor for OST-AR.

Three steps

  1. Experiment — Run psychophysics to reveal how the HVS shapes visibility under OST-AR.
  2. Extension — Add processing (mainly at the model's input stage) to reflect those findings.
  3. Validation — Compare human ratings against the model's predictions.

Related Work (4): Fukiage's Visibility Predictor

The model assumes a single 2D display — not OST-AR.

Four OST-AR-specific gaps:

  • ① Accommodation  —  induces defocus blur.
  • ② Binocular disparity  —  arises with depth; fusion is also a challenge.
  • ③ BG discounting  —  observers partly ignore the real BG.
  • ④ Optical addition  —  linear summation yields HDR-range light.

→ This work bridges these gaps, extending the model into an OST-AR visibility predictor.

Fukiage & Oishi, ACM TAP 2023

② Disparity & fusion
Geometric computation & existing-model selection.

④ BG discounting
Fit the attenuation process.

Pilot Experiment: Discussion

Four redesign directions for the main experiment.
① Drop weak-masking conditions
Discrete rating lacks resolution where the true effect is small.
② Fix luminance & SF
High YBG, low (but non-floor) YFG; low SF; reduce other axes.
③ Switch to matching method
Replace discrete rating with contrast-matching for finer resolution.
④ Add single-plane control
Simulate linear addition & defocus blur; compare against OST-AR.

Conclusion
